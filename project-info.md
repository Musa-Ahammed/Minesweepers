# 🧭 Minesweepers – Autonomous Demining Robot with Edge Data Logging & Remote Monitoring

Mine Finder is a field-ready prototype for autonomous detection of landmines using embedded systems and IoT integration. Built on Arduino, it combines metal detection, obstacle navigation, real-time location tagging, and remote alerting via LoRa/Bluetooth.

---

## 🚀 Features

- Autonomous pathfinding using ultrasonic-based obstacle detection
- Real-time metal detection simulating buried landmines
- GPS-based geo-tagging of detection events
- LoRa and Bluetooth support for real-time remote alerts
- Data logging to SD card for field record-keeping
- LCD Display for real-time robot status
- Modular design: sensors can be swapped or extended

Minesweepers is an international robotic competition on humanitarian de-mining that aims at motivating researchers and developers to work on innovative solutions for this serious problem. Students and researchers from all over the world compete in a two-phase contest to represent their worth to the whole world.
Under the leadership of KM Fahim Mahmud, a group of students from BRACU are competing in Minesweeper 2019. The team, named Mineceptor, features students from different disciplines and of many talents. Nefaur Rahman Labib and Mashiat Mamun Raidah designed the prototype, while Raihan Rahman managed the whole progression. The mechanical department consisting of Ashik Adnan, Hasibul Alam and Musa Ahammed Mahin built the chassis of the robot out of stainless steel pipes for structural integrity. The rover can carry the weight of an adult human being. The use of track instead of the traditional wheel system helps the rover move smoothly on rough terrain and helps distribute weight more evenly. A sturdy and efficient robotic arm with two-degree freedom has been incorporated with the rover, allowing the arm to stretch up to almost two feet from the base. Firoz Wadud and Fahin Uddin supervised the electronic department of the rover and devised their own custom made mine detector, which is able to detect both buried and surface mines. Touky Tahmid and Seyam Rabbi implemented the communication system which is capable of transmitting across 400 metres. Hasibul Islam coded a 20x20 matrix grid using python to auto-generate a virtual map after mines are detected. They are also using GPS as a backup system.
Minesweeper is a two phase competition, but the team has already gotten past the first phase with a whopping score of 74 points. The final phase is going to be held at the largest and the most impactful robotics research conference worldwide, the IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS 2019). It is going to take place at The Venetian Macao, Macau, China on November 4-8, 2019. But, the IROS participation fee is $200 per person, and the team will also have to fund their travel and accommodation expenses. 
Without proper funding, Team Mineceptor’s journey will come to an abrupt and disheartening end. They are actively seeking sponsorship to carry on to the final phase, and BRACU Express would like to support them by spreading word of their success and the great work they have done so far. We wish Team Mineceptor all the very best in their future endeavours and hope their dreams are not stalled for something as crude as a lack of funds.
